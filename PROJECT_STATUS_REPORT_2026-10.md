# Nexora AI
## Project Implementation and Placement Preparation Status Report

**Prepared:** 10 October 2026  
**Project:** Nexora AI career guidance and placement preparation platform  
**Report purpose:** Source-based project overview for project documentation and journal-paper preparation  
**Scope:** Public landing page, account entry and onboarding, career-development features, and the 18 placement-preparation requirement areas

---

## 1. Executive summary

Nexora AI is a web application that brings together career guidance, skills and career discovery, resume tools, interview preparation, learning recommendations, and placement preparation. The application has two connected product surfaces:

1. **General career-development workspace:** industry insights, career discovery, resume building and optimization, cover letters, interview quizzes, roadmaps, course recommendations, job matching, and career chat.
2. **Placement-preparation workspace:** a student profile, assessment, evidence-based readiness analysis, preparation plans, practice, adaptive levels, company and role preparation, mock interviews, interview evaluation, integrity-event review, job tracking, learning roadmaps, and a placement journey.

The codebase contains working user-interface routes and server-side workflows for these areas. It should **not** be described as a fully completed or empirically validated AI placement system. Features differ in maturity: some have concrete persistence and server-side validation, some provide a first functional workflow, and some depend on external services or student-entered information. The current placement readiness score is based on saved evidence and is explicit about unknown values; general role benchmarks are not employer-verified criteria.

The landing page presents broad promotional claims, including “50+ Industries Covered,” “1000+ Interview Questions,” and “95% Success Rate.” These are static display values in the page, and this source review did not find accompanying study data or validation for them. They must not be used as measured findings in a journal paper without independent supporting evidence.

The latest recorded verification for the placement-preparation changes is: eight deterministic Node unit tests passed, targeted ESLint completed with zero errors, and a production Next.js build completed. These checks establish code/build status only. They do not establish database-backed isolation, complete browser workflows, deployment operation, AI quality, placement outcomes, or user-study effectiveness.

## 3. Product overview: from the home page into placement preparation

### 3.1 Public landing page

The root page (`app/page.jsx`) renders a hero, feature cards, headline metrics, a “How it works” section, testimonials, FAQs, and a closing call to action. The hero describes Nexora as an AI career coach and presents “Start Preparation,” “Get Started,” and “Watch Demo” actions.

- “Start Preparation” routes signed-in users to the placement-preparation dashboard and presents a Clerk sign-in modal to signed-out users.
- “Get Started” links to the general industry-insights dashboard.
- “Watch Demo” currently has an empty URL in the source, so it is a visible placeholder rather than a functioning demo link.
- The displayed industry/question/success figures are static marketing copy. No analytical dataset, sample size, calculation, or study method was found for those figures.
- Testimonials are rendered from a local data file. Their presence is a presentation feature, not proof of independently verified user outcomes.

The public home page therefore introduces the two workspaces, but does not itself demonstrate that all described features have been validated or integrated.

### 3.2 Account creation and navigation

Clerk supplies sign-up and sign-in. The application wraps routes with Clerk’s provider and uses Clerk middleware for protected routes. The general header conditionally presents signed-in navigation to the dashboard, growth tools, and career chat. The placement-preparation area uses its own navigation and content layout.

For the placement workflow, the student account is associated with a separate preparation profile. Server actions resolve the authenticated Clerk identity, use the verified Clerk email, validate profile input, and prevent a conflicting email from being linked to a different Clerk account. Profile updates preserve fields omitted from a partial update while allowing an intentional clear.

### 3.3 General career-development workflow

The broader career workspace provides the following implemented route families:

- **Onboarding and dashboard:** onboarding captures information used by the authenticated dashboard and industry-insights view.
- **Industry insights:** dashboard data is retrieved through server actions and stored through Prisma-backed application models.
- **Career discovery:** a guided wizard supports a profile, career matches, skill-gap view, roadmap, course suggestions, and a weekly plan.
- **Resume and job materials:** resume builder, resume upload/analysis, ATS-oriented feedback, resume optimization, and AI cover-letter workflows are present in the route tree and related server actions.
- **Interview practice:** an interview quiz/workflow and performance-related screens are present. This is distinct from a verified professional hiring interview or externally administered assessment.
- **Roadmaps and learning:** roadmap and course recommendation routes exist, with API endpoints for roadmap and course workflows.
- **Career chat:** a chat interface and server routes are present, including session and upload routes.
- **Job matching and company exploration:** pages are present for job matching and company exploration; the repository does not demonstrate a synchronized live employer job feed.

The project README states that Gemini is used for several AI-backed functions and Inngest is used for selected asynchronous workflows. These features depend on deployment configuration and provider availability; no claim is made here that every external call was executed as part of the tests in this report.

### 3.4 Placement-preparation journey

The placement workspace is reached through `/placement-prep`. Its route redirects to the student dashboard. A typical intended student path is:

1. Sign in with Nexora and open the placement workspace.
2. Create or complete the placement student profile.
3. Complete the diagnostic assessment.
4. Generate readiness analysis from available assessment and practice evidence.
5. Review skills, evidence coverage, general role comparisons, and preparation priorities.
6. Generate a preparation plan and complete learning/practice tasks.
7. Use the practice center, adaptive levels, DSA exercises, and company/role preparation.
8. Practice an interview; review its evaluation and any integrity events separately.
9. Track saved jobs/applications and view the consolidated placement journey.

Some access is deliberately gated. For example, the dashboard only exposes the practice center after its profile/assessment/readiness/preparation completion conditions are met. These workflow gates do not mean that every feature has comprehensive automated end-to-end tests.

## 4. Technical architecture

### 4.1 Application stack

- **Web framework:** Next.js App Router (version reported by the recent build: 16.2.3).
- **UI:** React 19, JavaScript/JSX, Tailwind CSS, reusable UI components, Lucide icons, and Recharts for chart-oriented screens.
- **Authentication:** Clerk.
- **Persistence:** PostgreSQL through Prisma ORM (Prisma 6.19.3 in the recent build).
- **AI/model integration:** Google Gemini SDK dependencies are present; application documentation identifies Gemini-backed feature areas.
- **Background work:** Inngest dependency and routes are present.
- **Browser notifications:** optional Web Push via `web-push`, with VAPID configuration and a scheduled reminder route.
- **Validation/testing:** Zod schemas, ESLint, and Node's built-in test runner.
- **Deployment configuration:** Vercel-oriented configuration is present; actual deployment health is not established by this source report.

### 4.2 Request and data flow

The recurring application pattern is: React page/component -> Next.js server action or API route -> authenticated identity check -> input validation and record-ownership check -> Prisma read/write -> optional AI or background provider call -> result returned to the UI. Prisma models link student profiles to assessments, practice attempts, readiness records, plans, interviews, jobs, and journey activity.

The database schema is PostgreSQL-specific and includes both the general career-product models and placement-preparation models. The repository uses Prisma schema synchronization (`prisma db push`) rather than checked-in Prisma migration files, as documented in the README. Schema changes should be reviewed and backed up before applying them to any deployment database.

### 4.3 Scoring and evidence approach

Placement readiness uses a versioned score formula (`placement-score-v3`). It combines available, measured score components and renormalizes weights over those components. Unknown inputs, such as resume readiness without supported evidence, remain unknown instead of being silently converted to zero. Evidence aggregation uses saved assessment results and question-level practice answers, with topic and difficulty summaries where available. Readiness snapshots preserve historical scores and formula versions.

Role-fit comparisons use general role requirements in application data. They report evidence coverage, measured matches, measured gaps, and unknown skill areas. They are not employer-approved rubrics, labor-market predictions, or hiring probabilities.

## 5. Placement-preparation modules: implementation status

The following table aligns the user's 18 requirement areas with the current workspace. “Partial” means there is a feature foundation, not that the requirement is complete end-to-end.

### Module 1 — Authentication and student profile

**Current status: Partial / core workflow implemented.** Clerk authentication and placement profile creation/editing are present. The profile collects student and career-preference information; profile validation and preservation of fields during partial updates are implemented. The profile identity uses the verified sign-in email.  
**Remaining:** database-backed tests for persistence, ownership, conflicting accounts, and concurrent updates; verify every desired academic/project/certification/resume field is presented and retained across the complete browser workflow.

### Module 2 — Career and interest assessment

**Current status: Partial / diagnostic assessment implemented.** The fixed assessment includes career-interest choices and scored aptitude, programming, DSA, technical, and communication sections. Server-side validation checks completion and answer options against canonical question IDs; saved attempts carry a question-bank version. Browser integrity events are collected separately from answer scoring.  
**Remaining:** adaptive question selection, expanded and reviewed question bank, validation of question quality and assessment reliability, and full workflow/database tests. A fixed diagnostic bank is not yet an adaptive assessment system.

### Module 3 — Placement readiness and skill analysis

**Current status: Partial / evidence-aware scoring implemented.** Readiness combines saved assessment and practice evidence, exposes evidence coverage and skill gaps, records score snapshots and formula version, and avoids treating unmeasured skills as measured failures.  
**Remaining:** employer-specific verified skill benchmarks, independent validation of the scoring formula, database-backed history/ownership tests, and study evidence linking the score to real placement outcomes. Interview readiness is only available when an interview evaluation exists; resume readiness currently remains unknown.

### Module 4 — Personalized preparation engine

**Current status: Partial.** The product can generate weekly preparation plans and tasks based on readiness priorities and track task completion.  
**Remaining:** automatically re-prioritize active/future plan tasks when new performance evidence changes; establish plan-quality metrics and verify task sequencing and progress persistence.

### Module 5 — Assessment and practice center

**Current status: Partial / practice workflow implemented.** Practice routes cover aptitude, reasoning, verbal, coding/DSA, SQL and technical content. Practice submissions are validated against canonical question data and scored server-side.  
**Remaining:** broaden and quality-check question coverage, timed and company-pattern test depth, content maintenance, and automated tests across every practice mode.

### Module 6 — Progressive level system

**Current status: Partial.** Adaptive-learning screens and level evaluation exist. The system avoids assigning a proficiency level when there is no attempt evidence, and practice evidence can be separated by skill/topic.  
**Remaining:** demonstrate robust progression rules across repeated attempts and all relevant assessment sources; prove that level changes and consistency tracking persist correctly.

### Module 7 — Intelligent mistake analysis

**Current status: Partial.** Practice and assessment records retain answer-level data from which wrong answers and weak topics can be identified. A mistake-analysis feature route/action exists.  
**Remaining:** validate reliable distinction between conceptual, syntax, calculation, and time-management mistakes; demonstrate repeated-error detection and automatic targeted remedial practice with tests.

### Module 8 — Company and role preparation

**Current status: Partial.** Target role/company profile data and company/role preparation screens support comparisons and preparation tracking. General role evidence can inform comparisons.  
**Remaining:** verified and maintained company-specific test/interview patterns and role requirements; clearly source any company-specific claims. Current general benchmarks must not be represented as company requirements.

### Module 9 — AI roadmap generator

**Current status: Partial.** Learning roadmap routes and actions support generation, sequencing, milestones, and progress. Career-roadmap APIs also exist in the general workspace.  
**Remaining:** verify adaptive updates when new student evidence arrives, roadmap history/versioning, and real persistence/access behavior through integration tests.

### Module 10 — Course and learning recommendation

**Current status: Partial.** Course recommendation screens and API/action workflows exist in both the general career area and placement learning area; learning-roadmap screens expose resource recommendations.  
**Remaining:** verify resource quality, availability, cost, and links; ensure recommendations change in response to progress and maintain traceable recommendation provenance. Do not claim live course-provider synchronization unless implemented.

### Module 11 — Resume intelligence

**Current status: Partial.** The broader application has resume building, upload, parsing/analysis routes, ATS-oriented feedback, and role optimization workflows. The placement job area links to resume capabilities.  
**Remaining:** validate extraction across real-world resume formats, privacy/data-retention handling, and assessment quality against known ATS/employer systems. ATS scores are estimates, not guarantees of ranking or employment.

### Module 12 — AI mock interview agent

**Current status: Partial.** Interview sessions, personalized questions/follow-ups, saved transcripts, and optional browser speech input/output are documented and represented in routes/actions.  
**Remaining:** browser speech is not a real-time voice provider; speech support varies by browser. A provider-backed voice experience, audio storage/consent policy, and robust interruption/recovery validation are not demonstrated.

### Module 13 — AI interview evaluation

**Current status: Partial.** Completed interview sessions can have saved evaluation scores and evidence-linked feedback. Evaluation evidence can contribute to interview readiness.  
**Remaining:** validate rubric consistency and fairness, prove answer relevance/problem-solving/communication judgments with human review, and implement reliable audio-based metrics such as filler words or speech timing only if suitable audio evidence is available.

### Module 14 — Interview integrity and anti-malpractice

**Current status: Partial / event logging implemented with important limits.** Browser focus, visibility, copy/paste, context-menu, fullscreen, and camera/microphone track events are recorded. Integrity history is kept separate from performance scoring; officer review access is allowlisted server-side. No audio/video is recorded or uploaded for the described assessment flow.  
**Remaining:** browser events are not proof of identity or misconduct and can be incomplete or spoofed. Validate reporting, event persistence, access controls, and review procedures. Do not describe this as comprehensive proctoring.

### Module 15 — Job recommendation and matching

**Current status: Partial.** Job matching screens compare available profile/resume evidence with job details. The broader app includes a job matcher and placement job feature.  
**Remaining:** a live, maintained job feed and employer verification are not demonstrated. Matching quality depends on supplied job descriptions and student profile evidence; eligibility and missing skills are not externally verified.

### Module 16 — Job application tracker

**Current status: Partial / student-managed tracking.** Saved/application statuses and application history are supported in the placement workspace. Upcoming saved deadlines can contribute to reminders.  
**Remaining:** no ATS/employer status synchronization is demonstrated. Verify all status transitions, reminders, and privacy/access controls with database-backed tests.

### Module 17 — Placement journey tracker

**Current status: Partial.** The journey view aggregates Nexora-saved readiness, preparation/learning, practice, interviews, applications, and milestones as a read-oriented student progress view. Engagement preferences and optional reminders exist.  
**Remaining:** external application, shortlist, offer, and placement events are not independently confirmed. Validate aggregation correctness, empty states, and notification delivery in deployment.

### Module 18 — Student progress dashboard

**Current status: Partial.** The placement dashboard surfaces profile/setup status, assessment evidence, latest assessment score, and links to preparation features. Related screens show readiness, skill, plan, level, interview, and journey data.  
**Remaining:** complete and verify all requested daily/weekly trends, streaks, milestones, and cross-module statistics. No user telemetry or evaluation study is included in the current test evidence.

## 6. Cross-cutting status and known constraints

### 6.1 External integrations and configuration

- **Email/SMS:** no provider is configured according to the project README.
- **Browser push:** optional Web Push is implemented as a configured path; deployment requires VAPID keys, a server-only encryption key, a cron secret, HTTPS, and a compatible browser. Actual production delivery is not verified by this report.
- **Voice interview:** uses browser `SpeechRecognition` / `speechSynthesis`; no configured real-time audio-provider integration is documented.
- **LeetCode / code judge:** DSA links and solve status are student-reported; no LeetCode synchronization or server-side coding judge is documented.
- **Jobs and employers:** no synchronized live job feed, employer account workflow, or ATS application-status integration is documented.
- **Company knowledge:** comparisons depend on local/general role benchmark data; employer-approved company pattern sources are not established.

### 6.2 Testing and validation limits

The repository's `npm test` suite contains eight deterministic tests for score weighting, unknown evidence, formula versioning, evidence aggregation, mixed practice evidence, adaptive levels without evidence, and profile partial-update behavior. Recent targeted ESLint reported no errors. A recent production build compiled successfully and generated static pages.

The suite does not include a configured database-backed integration-test framework or end-to-end browser test suite for the student journey. No load test, accessibility audit, AI-output benchmark, security penetration test, deployment smoke test, or student usability/learning-outcome trial is evidenced by these checks. The Node test run reports a non-fatal module-type warning because the package is not configured as ESM while some test imports use ESM syntax.

### 6.3 Product claims and research validity

For a journal paper, distinguish **designed functionality** from **evaluated outcomes**. In particular:

- Do not report home-page headline metrics as empirical project results without source data and method.
- Do not claim the platform improves placement probability, academic performance, or employability without a controlled or otherwise defensible evaluation.
- Describe role-fit and readiness scores as application-generated indicators based on available evidence, not validated predictors.
- Explain the limitations of AI evaluation, recommendation bias, missing evidence, browser variability, and external data freshness.
- Avoid calling integrity-event monitoring “proctoring” that establishes misconduct; describe exactly which client-side signals are logged and what is not collected.
- State the model/provider configuration and evaluation date for any reported AI output. Preserve human review for high-impact decisions.

## 7. Recommended completion plan

The following order minimizes risk and makes the product easier to evaluate:

1. **Establish a reproducible test baseline:** configure database-backed tests with test-only data, add ownership/authorization tests, and add browser tests for the full profile-to-journey path.
2. **Finish core learning loop:** connect topic-level evidence to preparation-plan generation and automatically reprioritize future tasks after new results.
3. **Validate assessments and levels:** review question quality, record bank versions, measure reliability, test level promotion/demotion, and add a documented question-adaptation policy.
4. **Strengthen evidence and evaluation:** retain provenance for every recommendation and score, validate interview rubric decisions, and test empty/unknown/stale evidence cases.
5. **Complete tracking and integrations:** decide whether live jobs, employer status sync, voice services, or email/SMS are in scope; obtain required provider approval/configuration before claiming these capabilities.
6. **Privacy, accessibility, and deployment checks:** review retention/consent for uploaded resumes and transcripts, keyboard/screen-reader use, security boundaries, Vercel environment variables, cron delivery, and production database migrations.
7. **Conduct a research study before outcome claims:** define research questions, participant population, consent, baseline/comparison, task protocol, outcome measures, analysis plan, limitations, and ethics review as appropriate.

## 10. Conclusion

Nexora AI has a substantial implemented foundation: a public career-coaching entry point, authenticated general career features, and an 18-area placement-preparation workspace. The strongest recent engineering work is in validated profile/assessment/practice submissions and evidence-aware readiness scoring that preserves unknowns rather than inventing unsupported measurements. However, feature presence is not equivalent to full completion, external integration, or proven effectiveness. The most important gaps are comprehensive integration/E2E testing, adaptive plan/question behavior, validated assessment and interview measures, employer/job data integration, and an empirical student evaluation. A journal paper should present the platform as an implemented prototype or system, report its tested scope precisely, and reserve effectiveness claims for a properly designed study.
